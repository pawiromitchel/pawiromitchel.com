Scraping 8 Surinamese banks without losing my mind

None of the banks in Suriname have a rates API, so I built ForexSU to scrape them. Notes on parsers that survive redesigns, serving stale data on purpose, and why it's a PWA.

Mitchel Pawirodinomo

Technical Operations Engineer at QuickNode

  • typescript
  • pwa
  • web-scraping
  • node
  • real-world

In Suriname, USD and EUR exchange rates move every day, and every bank sets its own. If you want to change money, send a wire or pay an invoice abroad, you have to shop around. None of the banks offer an API.

So in practice, checking rates meant opening eight websites one after another:

  • Central Bank of Suriname (CBVS)
  • De Surinaamsche Bank (DSB)
  • Hakrinbank
  • Republic Bank
  • Finabank
  • VCB
  • Southern Commercial Bank (SCB)
  • CME

On mobile data, several of those take a while to load. I got tired of it and built ForexSU: one page with every bank's rates, refreshed every five minutes, that you can install on your phone.

The page itself was the easy part. Keeping scrapers alive against websites I don't control was not.

Bank websites change all the time

The first version of a scraper usually looks like this:

And it breaks within a couple of weeks. The banks redesign their sites, a column moves, someone switches from 38.50 to 38,50, or the site returns a 500 for twenty minutes during the morning rush.

What ended up working was to stop caring about where the table is on the page. The parser looks at every table row, keeps the ones that mention USD or EUR, and pulls out the numbers that could plausibly be exchange rates:

The range check does more work than it looks like. Phone numbers, dates, years in the footer and account numbers all fall outside 10–100, so they're gone before anything tries to interpret them. The downside is that the range is hardcoded. If the SRD ever goes past 100 to the dollar, the bounds need updating.

Show old data rather than no data

If one bank's site is down at 10 AM, the page should still show that bank. It just shouldn't pretend the numbers are fresh.

When a scrape fails, ForexSU serves the last rates that worked and labels them, for example "Cached 25 mins ago". Rates from half an hour ago are still useful. An empty card isn't.

Why it's a PWA

Most people check rates on their phone, often on a slow connection. Making the site a PWA with a stale-while-revalidate service worker helped in three ways:

  1. It opens instantly. The rates from your last visit come straight out of the Cache Storage API.
  2. It updates itself. The service worker fetches fresh rates from /api/rates in the background and the page updates when they arrive.
  3. It installs like an app. "Add to Home Screen" on iOS or Android gives you an icon, no app store involved.

Looking back

Solving a small, local problem like this is the kind of project I enjoy most. None of the individual pieces is clever: a forgiving parser, a cache that's willing to serve old data, and a small PWA. Together they're enough to keep an app useful while the sites it depends on keep changing.

It's live at forexsu.co.
